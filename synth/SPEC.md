# Spec for the synthetic panel generator

Goal: a generator of synthetic monthly count panels on a grid, with planted time-lagged links from
input series to counts, and null panels with none. It will test a forecasting method that you are
deliberately not told about. Your independence from that method is the point of this work.

## Limits

- Do **not** open `docs/`, `results/`, `src/`, `scripts/`, `tests/` or `data/panel/counts.parquet`,
  nor `data/raw/`. Ignore any description of methods you meet elsewhere in the repo.
- Write only in `synth/`: `generate.py`, `check_shape.py` and `README.md`. Do not edit this file.
  Do not commit, push, install anything or use the network.
- Import only numpy and the standard library in `generate.py`. `check_shape.py` may also use duckdb.
  Nothing may import from `src` or `causal_atlas`.
- Budget: about 100k tokens. If calibration is not converging at about 80k, stop and report where it
  stands, honestly, rather than continuing. Long runs: prefix with `nice -n 19`.
- Run Python with `~/.local/bin/uv run python`, from the repo root.

## Interface

```python
generate(panel, lat, lon, seed) -> dict
```

- `panel`: one of `"planted_1.05"`, `"planted_1.15"`, `"planted_1.40"`, `"pure_null"`, `"shared_null"`.
- `lat`, `lon`: 1-D arrays of cell centres in degrees, on a 0.5 degree grid. It must work for any
  subset of cells. The full grid is in `data/panel/cells.parquet` (columns `cell, lat, lon, country`).
- Fixed 435 months, index 0 = January 1989, index 434 = March 2025. Calendar month is index mod 12.
- Deterministic in `seed`. A full-size call on 10,228 cells takes under about 30 seconds and under
  about 2 GB.

Returns numpy arrays, with `C` cells and `T` = 435 months:

| Key | Shape | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `y` | C by T, integer | Counts |
| `drought` | C by T, float | A gridded input. NaN for nothing unless you have a reason |
| `oni` | T, float | One global input series |
| `rate` | C by T, float | The conditional mean used to draw `y[:, t]`, given the past |
| `rate_no_link` | C by T, float | The same with the planted link's contribution removed, on the same history. Equal to `rate` on null panels |
| `linked` | C, bool | Cells with a planted link. All False on null panels |

Every `y[:, t]` depends only on the past of `y` and on inputs at t or before.

## Mechanism

A self-exciting negative binomial count process over a latent spatial rate:

- A latent log rate that is spatially clustered, with most cells close to never having an event.
- Self-excitation: past counts in the cell and nearby raise the rate, decaying over time.
- Regional episodes: multi-year, spatially extended raised-rate periods, unrelated to any input.
- A mild seasonal cycle.
- Negative binomial draws, with the mean given by `rate` and a dispersion you calibrate.

**Planted panels.** `planted_R`: the link adds to the log rate through a threshold effect of the
inputs over distributed lags you choose, in only some cells. Choose all of this yourself and not
for convenience: which share of cells, where they are, which lags and weights, what threshold, how
the link divides between `drought` and `oni`. Avoid simple rolling-window means as the lag shape: use
something irregular, with some effect at lags beyond a few months and little at the shortest lags.
Define R so that a linked cell's rate multiplies by R for each standard deviation that the
lag-weighted input lies past the threshold. Use the same design at every R, so only R changes. Record
your choices in `synth/README.md`.

**`pure_null`.** No links. The inputs are drawn independently of the counts. The regional episodes
must be large enough to be a hard case: a persistent input can line up with a persistent episode by
chance.

**`shared_null`.** As `pure_null`, and in addition the inputs and the counts share a seasonal driver
and a slow regional trend, with no link from inputs to counts. This should be a realistic trap for a
method that mistakes a shared driver for a link.

**Inputs in every panel.** `drought` is shaped like a standardised drought index: spatially smooth and
temporally persistent. `oni` is shaped like an El Nino index: strongly persistent over months, with
cycles of several years. Multi-year persistence must exist in both, via a slow component.

## Targets

Measured on a real panel, in training months only: months 36 to 338 of the fixed index, 303 months.
Calibrate on `pure_null` at full size on those months. Hit the first group closely and the second
in order of magnitude. Do not expect to match all at once; say what you could not match.

**Counts, first group**

| Statistic | Real value |
|---|---|
| Mean count per cell-month | 0.0097 |
| Share of zero cell-months | 0.9953 |
| Variance over mean, pooled | 24.8 |
| Share of cells with any event | 0.210 |
| Lag-1 autocorrelation, pooled over cells and months | 0.372 |
| Lag-12 autocorrelation, pooled | 0.061 |
| Share of events in the top 0.1% of cell-months | 0.559 |
| Share of events in the top 1% of cells | 0.550 |

**Counts, second group**

| Statistic | Real value |
|---|---|
| Largest single cell-month | 609 |
| Share of events in the top 0.01% of cell-months | 0.214 |
| Peak over trough of the mean by calendar month, all cells | 1.99 |
| Moran's I of log(1 + cell total), 8-neighbour graph | 0.555 |
| Episode length in months, median, 90th percentile, max (below) | 1, 4, 46 |

An episode: in a 5 by 5 degree block, block edges at multiples of 5, keeping blocks with at least 12
events over the training months, a run of consecutive months over twice that block's own mean.

**Inputs**

## Deliver

- `synth/generate.py`: the generator, plain and readable.
- `synth/check_shape.py`: prints each target beside its value for one replicate, over the training
  months, and for `planted_1.40` prints the share of linked cells and the share of events in them.
- `synth/README.md`: your design choices, what you did not match, and why.

End with a report of at most 15 lines: files written, the table's fit, runtime, memory, tokens as
you can estimate them, and anything you are unsure of. State failures plainly.
