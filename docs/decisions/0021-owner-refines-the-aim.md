## His words

"The idea is basically that what we're trying to do is we're trying to look at given a whole bunch of factors, and we don't know what those factors are, could be, so it could be weather, it could be drought, it could be crime, it could be house prices, it could be pollen counts, it could be any number of things. And then it's to try and find combinations of all of these things, some of these things, or any number of these things that might actually help us to predict or infer a direction. And this could be very broad and very wide. And the idea is that we may actually find certain combinations produce an impact without us ever thinking of those combinations. So it's different from having a hypothesis which says, oh, you know, if there's drought, then there's going to be a shortage of cocoa in that area, and therefore the cocoa prices are going to go up. But that could be one of the things we look at as well. [...] who would have thought that these 7 or 8 different things, or 50 different things, or 100 different factors over a period of time at a certain way, well, there is a correlation there. There is something collectively. So that's what we're trying to aim towards. [...] I don't have the answers as to how we do this yet. That's what we're trying to figure out."
